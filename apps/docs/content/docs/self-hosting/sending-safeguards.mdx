---
title: Sending safeguards
description: Which sending limits and checks apply to a self-hosted install, and how to change them.
---

SendLit Cloud runs checks that protect shared infrastructure from new,
unknown accounts. A self-hosted install (`SENDLIT_DEPLOYMENT_MODE=oss`, the
default in `.env.example`) trusts its own users, so most of those checks are
off. This page lists what applies where.

| Safeguard | Self-hosted | SendLit Cloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test-send limits | On | On |
| Your ESP's own open and click tracking turned off | On | On |
| Signup guards (disposable emails, signups per IP) | Off | On |
| Content scan before sending | Off | On |
| Review of a new account's large import | Off | On |
| Review of a new account's first large broadcast through its own ESP | Off | On |
| Pacing of a new account's sends through a shared mailbox | Off | On |
| New-account daily ramp and monthly send caps (plans) | Off | On |
| Auto-pause on high bounce or complaint rates | On | On |

Variables go in the root `.env` file; the Compose stack passes all of it to
the API. The full list, with defaults, is in `apps/api/.env.example`.

## Test-send limits

**Send test** in the editor sends real email, so it's limited on every
install. Each recipient counts once, over rolling windows:

| Variable | Default | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TEST_SEND_DAILY_LIMIT` | 100 | Test emails per team in any 24 hours |
| `TEST_SEND_BURST_LIMIT` | 50 | Test emails per team in any `TEST_SEND_BURST_HOURS` |
| `TEST_SEND_BURST_HOURS` | 3 | Length of the burst window (at most 24) |
| `TEST_SEND_EMAIL_DAILY_LIMIT` | 20 | Test emails of one email in any 24 hours |

The counters live in Redis, so they hold across API restarts and replicas.

## Your ESP's own tracking

SendLit tracks opens and clicks itself. If the ESP tracked the same email,
every link would be wrapped a second time behind the ESP's domain, and clicks
would be counted twice. SendLit adds a header to each tracked email that turns
the ESP's tracking off:

| ESP | What SendLit does |
| --- | --- |
| SendGrid | `X-SMTPAPI` with the `clicktrack` and `opentrack` filters off. SendGrid's click tracking is on by default, so this matters most here. |
| Mailgun | `X-Mailgun-Track-Clicks: no` and `X-Mailgun-Track-Opens: no` |
| Postmark | `X-PM-TrackOpens: false` and `X-PM-TrackLinks: None` |
| Resend | No per-email switch: turn off open and click tracking on the domain in Resend. |
| Amazon SES | No per-email switch: don't add open or click events to the configuration set SendLit sends through. |
| Other SMTP | Nothing to do. |

This applies to broadcasts, sequences and test sends, and to transactional
emails where SendLit tracks opens or clicks. Headers a transactional request
sets itself take precedence.

## Auto-pause

A broadcast or sequence pauses itself when its bounce or complaint rate gets
too high, or when the ESP keeps failing. This runs on every install:

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `AUTO_PAUSE_MIN_SENT` | 100 | Emails sent before rates are judged |
| `AUTO_PAUSE_BOUNCE_BPS` | 200 | Bounce rate that pauses, in basis points (200 = 2%) |
| `AUTO_PAUSE_COMPLAINT_BPS` | 8 | Complaint rate that pauses (8 = 0.08%) |
| `AUTO_PAUSE_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES` | 5 | Failed sends in a row that pause |
| `AUTO_PAUSE_CHECK_SECONDS` | 30 | How often a sending campaign's rates are rechecked |

Rates need bounce and complaint feedback from your ESP; see
[Amazon SES bounces and complaints](/self-hosting/amazon-ses-feedback) for SES.

## Cloud-only checks

`SENDLIT_DEPLOYMENT_MODE=cloud` turns on the account checks in the table
above; `ABUSE_CONTROLS=off` turns them off again. They need SendLit Cloud's
billing setup and an operator to review flagged accounts with
`bun scripts/abuse.ts`, so they aren't meant for a self-hosted install. Their
variables (`SIGNUP_*`, `IMPORT_*`, `PACING_HOURS`,
`FIRST_BROADCAST_REVIEW_RECIPIENTS`, `CONTENT_SCAN`) are documented in
`apps/api/.env.example`, and what users see is described in
[Acceptable use](/workspace/acceptable-use).
