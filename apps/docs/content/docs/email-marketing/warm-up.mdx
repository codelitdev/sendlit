---
title: Warming up a domain or SES account
description: Ramp up sending on a new domain, IP or Amazon SES account without hurting deliverability.
---

Mailbox providers such as Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo judge mail by the
reputation of the sending domain and the IP addresses it comes from. A
domain or account with no history that suddenly sends tens of thousands of
emails looks like a spammer, so mail lands in spam or is throttled. Warming
up means building that history gradually: sending to your most engaged
readers first and raising the volume over two to four weeks.

SendLit doesn't warm up automatically yet. Until it does, the
**daily send limit** on an ESP lets you ramp up by hand.

## Do you need to warm up?

Usually less than you fear, if you keep what already has a reputation:

- **Same sending domain.** Gmail and Outlook weigh domain reputation heavily.
  If you sent from `news.example.com` with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up on your
  old platform (beehiiv, Mailchimp, Substack custom domains), keep sending
  from that domain. Its history comes with you.
- **Same ESP account or IP.** If you already send through your own Amazon SES,
  Postmark, SendGrid or Mailgun account, connect that account to SendLit.
  Nothing about your sending infrastructure changes.
- **A clean list.** Import your list with its statuses so unsubscribed,
  bounced and complained addresses stay suppressed (see
  [Import contacts](/email-marketing/contacts#import-contacts)).

You do need to warm up when you move to a **new domain**, a **new Amazon SES
account** or a **new dedicated IP**, or when your list is much bigger than
what you sent before. Even with an existing domain, a new SES account's
shared IPs and quota benefit from a short ramp.

## Amazon SES before you start

- **Leave the sandbox.** New SES accounts can only send to verified
  addresses. In the SES console, request production access for your region,
  describing your newsletter, how people subscribe and how you handle
  bounces and complaints.
- **Check your quotas.** SES gives each account a daily sending quota
  (`Max24HourSend`) and a maximum send rate per second (`MaxSendRate`). Set
  the ESP's **Max send rate** in SendLit to your account's `MaxSendRate`
  (see [Send rate](/email-marketing/settings#send-rate)), and ask AWS for a
  higher daily quota before your list outgrows it.
- **Send bounces and complaints back to SendLit**, so bad addresses are
  suppressed straight away. See
  [Amazon SES bounces and complaints](/self-hosting/amazon-ses-feedback).
- **Authenticate your domain** with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and verify it in SES.

## Set a daily send limit

Open **Settings → Email service providers**, edit the ESP and set
**Daily send limit for warm-up**. It caps how many broadcast and sequence
emails go through that ESP per UTC day. Emails over the cap aren't lost or
marked failed: they wait and go out from midnight UTC, and a large broadcast
simply takes several days. Transactional email (sign-in codes, receipts) is
never held back. The ESP list shows today's count against the limit.

Raise the limit every day or two as long as bounces and complaints stay low,
and clear it once you're sending your full volume. You can also set it with
`PATCH /settings/esps/{espId}` (`dailySendLimit`, or `null` to remove it) or
the MCP `update_esp` tool.

## A sample ramp

Adjust the steps to your list size; stop raising the limit if bounce or
complaint rates climb.

| Days | Daily send limit |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 | 500 |
| 3-4 | 1,000 |
| 5-6 | 2,500 |
| 7-8 | 5,000 |
| 9-10 | 10,000 |
| 11-14 | 20,000 |
| 15-21 | 50,000 |
| 22-28 | 100,000 or your full list |

## Send to your most engaged readers first

Early sends should go to people who open and click, because their engagement
builds reputation fastest. Readers who signed up recently are usually the
most engaged, so a segment of recent signups (**Signed up after** a date) is
a good first audience. If your old platform exports engagement, import it as
a tag or custom field and build a segment from it. Send your first issues to
that segment, then widen the audience as you raise the limit. Leave long
inactive readers until last, or consider not mailing them at all.

## Watch the numbers

- **Bounces:** keep the bounce rate under 2%. A hard bounce suppresses the
  address automatically; check the [Suppressions](/activity/suppressions)
  page after each send.
- **Spam complaints:** keep them under 0.1%. Gmail and Yahoo require
  bulk senders to stay below 0.3%.
- **Opens and clicks** on the broadcast's stats. A sudden drop often means
  mail is going to spam.
- **Google Postmaster Tools** and **Microsoft SNDS** show how those providers
  rate your domain and IPs.

If the numbers get worse, lower the daily limit, send only to your engaged
segment for a few days, and clean addresses that bounce.
