---
title: Signup forms
description: Let people subscribe themselves from a hosted page or a form on your website.
---

A signup form adds the people who fill it in to your contacts. Create one in
**Signup forms → New form**. Each form has:

- **Tags** added to everyone who subscribes through it, for example
  `newsletter`. Use them in segments, or start a sequence on the tag.
- **Double opt-in** (on by default). SendLit emails a confirmation link and
  subscribes the person only once they click it and press **Confirm
  subscription**. This keeps typos, fake addresses and spam traps off your
  list, which protects your sender reputation. The link is valid for three
  days.
- **Redirect after signup** (optional): an `http(s)` URL to send people to
  after they submit the form, and after they confirm. Without it, SendLit shows
  its own thank-you page.

New subscribers trigger "subscriber added" sequences, so a welcome sequence
starts on its own. The confirmation email is sent through your team's email
provider and appears in the transactional log.

## Share or embed a form

Every form has a hosted page at `https://<your SendLit API>/f/<formId>`. Open
it from the form's row, or link to it from anywhere.

To put the form on your own website, choose **Embed** and paste the HTML. Style
it as you like, but keep the field names: `email` (required), `name`
(optional) and the hidden `website` field, which catches spam bots.

```html
<form action="https://<your SendLit API>/f/frm_..." method="post">
  <input type="email" name="email" placeholder="you@example.com" required>
  <input type="text" name="name" placeholder="Your name">
  <input type="text" name="website" tabindex="-1" autocomplete="off"
    style="position:absolute;left:-9999px" aria-hidden="true">
  <button type="submit">Subscribe</button>
</form>
```

From JavaScript, post JSON to the same URL; it allows cross-origin requests:

```js
await fetch("https://<your SendLit API>/f/frm_...", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
  body: JSON.stringify({ email, name }),
});
// 200 { "status": "confirmation_sent" | "subscribed" }
// 400 { "error": "invalid_email" }, 429 { "error": "too_many_requests" }
```

## What SendLit filters out

The answer is the same whether or not an address was accepted, so a form
can't be used to find out who is on your list. SendLit quietly drops:

- addresses on your [suppression list](/activity/suppressions), and disposable
  email domains;
- submissions that fill in the hidden `website` field;
- more than two confirmation emails a day to one address.

Each form takes at most 10 submissions an hour from one network and 1,000 a
day in total; beyond that it answers `429`.

A single opt-in form subscribes new addresses straight away, but never
resubscribes someone who unsubscribed. They can come back through a double
opt-in form, because clicking the confirmation link proves it's them.

## API and MCP

Manage forms with the `/forms` endpoints in the [API reference](/api-reference)
or the `*_signup_form` MCP tools. The submission URL itself is public and
needs no API key.
