title: Sending Settings
description: Configure the team's email sending provider.
---

Each team can configure its own SMTP provider. The API never returns stored passwords or secrets.

Each ESP has a from name and from email, and an optional **reply-to** address.
When set, broadcasts, sequences and transactional email sent through the ESP
carry a `Reply-To` header, so replies reach that inbox (for example
`support@`) instead of a `noreply@` sender. A transactional send's own
`replyTo` takes precedence; a shared organization ESP can be given a per-team
reply-to on the team's grant.

## Port and TLS

Most providers accept SMTP on port **587**: leave **Use TLS** off and SendLit
upgrades the connection with STARTTLS, so it's still encrypted. Turn **Use TLS**
on only for port **465**, which expects encryption from the first byte.
Toggling **Use TLS** switches between 587 and 465 for you. Mixing them up (TLS
on port 587, or plain on 465) fails with an SSL "wrong version number" error or
a connection that never answers; the form and the test send say which to
change.

## Domain authentication

The shield button on an ESP checks SPF, DKIM and DMARC for its From
address's domain and lists the DNS records to add. See
[Domain authentication](/email-marketing/domain-authentication).

## Send rate

Providers cap how many emails per second an account may send; Amazon SES
starts new accounts at 14 per second and rejects anything faster with
`Throttling`. Set **Max send rate** on the ESP to your account's limit (for
SES, the `MaxSendRate` shown in the SES console). SendLit spaces every send
through that ESP to stay under it, across all workers, so a large broadcast
takes longer instead of failing. Left empty, SES ESPs use 14 per second,
Resend 2, and other providers aren't throttled.

If the provider still replies that it is throttling (for example SES's
`454 Throttling failure`) or asks to retry later, the email is rescheduled a
minute later (15 minutes for a daily quota) without counting as a failed
attempt.

## Daily send limit

**Daily send limit for warm-up** caps the broadcast and sequence emails sent
through the ESP per UTC day. Emails over the cap wait for the next day
instead of failing; transactional email is never held back. Use it to ramp up
a new domain or SES account, or turn on **Automatic warm-up** to have SendLit
raise the limit every day, most engaged contacts first; see
[Warming up a domain or SES account](/email-marketing/warm-up). Shared
organization ESPs have their own per-team daily and monthly quotas, set by an
organization admin; campaign mail over those quotas also waits until the
quota resets.

## Open and click tracking

SendLit tracks opens and clicks itself. If your ESP tracked the same email,
every link would be wrapped a second time, behind the ESP's domain, and
clicks would be counted twice. So SendLit tells SendGrid, Mailgun and Postmark
not to track its broadcasts, sequences and test sends, and transactional
emails where SendLit tracks opens or clicks. Resend and Amazon SES have no
per-email switch: turn off their open and click tracking for the domain
(Resend) or configuration set (SES) yourself.

## Testing

Use the test send endpoint after configuring settings to verify delivery before sending broadcasts or activating sequences.

## Bounce and complaint feedback

Each ESP can report bounces and spam complaints back to SendLit, which then suppresses those addresses automatically. Open the ESP's delivery feedback settings, copy its webhook URL into your provider, and enter the value the provider asks for. Resend, Postmark, SendGrid, and Mailgun use a webhook signing secret or key; Amazon SES uses an SNS topic ARN. See [Bounce and complaint feedback](/email-marketing/delivery-feedback) for each provider's setup.
