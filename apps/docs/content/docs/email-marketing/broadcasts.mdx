---
title: Broadcasts
description: One-time email sends.
---

A broadcast is a one-off send to subscribed contacts matching an audience filter. An empty filter (the default "Everyone" audience) sends to all subscribed contacts.

Use **Exclude** to leave contacts out even when they match the audience, for
example everyone tagged `newsletter` except those tagged `customer`. Over the
API and MCP this is the sequence's `excludeFilter`; send `null` to clear it.
The recipient count in the editor already accounts for exclusions.

Broadcasts accept only templates whose `purpose` is `marketing`. Selecting or
submitting a transactional template returns `422 template_not_marketing`.
Marketing templates contain one final managed footer. SendLit renders the
workspace mailing address and recipient-specific unsubscribe URL at delivery
time.

## Plain text and the web version

Every broadcast and sequence email is sent with a plain-text part generated
from the HTML, which spam filters and text-only mail clients expect.

The managed footer links a hosted web version of the email
("View in browser"). The link is signed and personal to the recipient, so the
web version shows their merge-tag data and their unsubscribe link. It has no
open or click tracking and isn't indexed by search engines. To place the link
elsewhere in your content, use the `{{ view_in_browser_url }}` merge tag.

The web version shows the email as it was sent. If you edit a sequence email
later, people who already got it still see the version they received, and a
test's link shows the email as you tested it.

Once a broadcast is [published to the web](/email-marketing/web-archive), the
footer link goes to its public page instead, and so does the
`{{ web_version_url }}` merge tag. Before that, `{{ web_version_url }}` is the
recipient's own web version.

### Turn the web version off

Some emails don't belong on the web, such as onboarding, trial or account
emails. Turn **View in browser** off on the broadcast or sequence (through the
API or MCP `update_sequence`, this is `viewInBrowser: false`). Its emails are
then sent without the footer link, `{{ view_in_browser_url }}` is empty, and
the web versions behind links already sent stop working. Turning it back on
restores them. It doesn't affect a broadcast's
[public page](/email-marketing/web-archive), which you publish separately.

## Automatic pause on bounces and complaints

While a broadcast or sequence sends, SendLit watches its bounce and spam
complaint rates. Once at least 100 of its emails went out, it pauses
automatically when more than 2% bounce or more than 0.08% are marked as spam.
It also pauses after five failed sends in a row, or straight away when your
email provider refuses to send at all (for example an account that is
suspended or a sender that isn't verified). Throttling never pauses it; those
emails just wait.

The broadcast's page then explains why and shows the bounce and complaint
rates, and the organization's owners get an email. Emails that haven't gone
out yet stay queued: clean your list or fix your provider, then choose
**Resume**. Rates after a resume start over. Self-hosters can tune the
thresholds with `AUTO_PAUSE_MIN_SENT`, `AUTO_PAUSE_BOUNCE_BPS`,
`AUTO_PAUSE_COMPLAINT_BPS` and `AUTO_PAUSE_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES`.

## Send a test

Use **Send test** in the broadcast editor (or the full-screen content editor)
to send yourself a copy before launching. It saves your changes first, then
sends the email exactly as recipients will get it (merge tags, footer,
tracking) with `[Test]` added to the subject, through the delivery source the
real send will use. It defaults to your own address and accepts up to five.
When the recipient is one of your contacts, merge tags use their data.
Opens, clicks and the unsubscribe link in a test change nothing, and
suppressed addresses are skipped.
The email's `lastTestSentAt` records when the last test went out.

### Test limits

Tests don't count towards your plan, so they have limits of their own, like
other email marketing platforms. Every address counts as one test:

- **100 tests per team** in any 24 hours, and no more than 50 in any 3 hours.
- **20 tests per email** in any 24 hours.

When you reach a limit, Send test says so; earlier tests age out of the
window over time. Tests sent through an organization's shared mailbox also
count against your team's shared-delivery quota, because the organization
pays for that mailbox. Tests through your team's own ESP don't.

Self-hosters can change the limits with `TEST_SEND_DAILY_LIMIT`,
`TEST_SEND_BURST_LIMIT`, `TEST_SEND_BURST_HOURS` and
`TEST_SEND_EMAIL_DAILY_LIMIT`.

Over the API: `POST /sequences/{sequenceId}/emails/{emailId}/test` with an
optional `{ "to": ["you@example.com"] }`. API keys have no signed-in user, so
they must pass `to`. The MCP tool is `send_sequence_email_test`.

## Unsubscribing

Opening the unsubscribe URL shows a confirmation page; the contact is
unsubscribed only when they confirm. Security scanners and mail clients that
prefetch links therefore can't unsubscribe anyone. A `POST` to the same URL
unsubscribes immediately, which is how mailbox providers' one-click
unsubscribe (RFC 8058) works.

Broadcast delivery uses the same sequence state machine as automations:

- `draft` means the broadcast is editable.
- `active` with a future send time means scheduled.
- `active` after the send time means sending.
- `completed` means sent.

The first email's `delayInMillis` stores the absolute send timestamp for broadcasts.
