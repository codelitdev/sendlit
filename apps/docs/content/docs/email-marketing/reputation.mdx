---
title: Sender reputation
description: See how Gmail and Outlook rate your mail with Google Postmaster Tools and Microsoft SNDS.
---

Your email provider reports mail as "delivered" once Gmail or Outlook accepts
it, even if it then lands in spam. Gmail and Outlook publish what they think
of your mail through free tools, and SendLit shows that data on the
**Sender reputation** card of the dashboard, with a 30-day trend.

## What you see

**Gmail (Google Postmaster Tools)**, per sending domain:

- **Spam rate**: the share of mail Gmail users marked as spam. Keep it below
  0.1%; Google may reject or junk mail from senders above **0.3%**.
- **SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass rates**: should be close to 100%; see
  [Domain authentication](/email-marketing/domain-authentication).
- **Delivery error rate**: mail Gmail rejected or deferred.
- **Meets Google's requirements**: Google's verdict on its bulk-sender rules
  (authentication, one-click unsubscribe, spam rate, ...).

Google retired its "domain reputation" and "IP reputation" grades in
Postmaster Tools v2, so SendLit shows the numbers above instead.

**Outlook (Microsoft SNDS)**, per IP address you send from:

- **Filter result** for each day: green (under 10% of mail filtered as spam),
  yellow (10% to 90%) or red (over 90%);
- **complaint rate** and **spam trap hits**.

SNDS covers IP addresses, so it's useful only if your email provider gives
you a dedicated IP. On shared IPs, rely on Postmaster Tools.

## Alerts

SendLit checks the data every day and shows alerts on the card. It also
emails your organization's owners when one starts or gets worse:

| Signal | Warning | Critical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gmail spam rate | 0.1% or more | 0.3% or more |
| Google sender requirements | Not met | |
| SNDS filter result | Yellow | Red |

When an alert appears, stop sending to contacts who haven't opened in months,
check your [authentication](/email-marketing/domain-authentication), and slow
down with a [daily send limit](/email-marketing/warm-up). Broadcasts and
sequences also [pause on their own](/email-marketing/broadcasts) when their
own bounce or complaint rates get too high.

## Connect Google Postmaster Tools

1. Open [Google Postmaster Tools](https://postmaster.google.com), add your
   sending domain (the part after `@` in your From address) and verify it with
   the DNS record Google shows.
2. In SendLit, choose **Connect Google Postmaster Tools** on the dashboard and
   sign in with the same Google account. SendLit asks only for read access to
   Postmaster Tools traffic data.
3. Data appears within a few minutes and refreshes daily. Google shows numbers
   only for days on which a domain sent enough mail to Gmail users, so a small
   list may show no data.

SendLit checks every domain your email providers send from. A domain marked
**Not in Postmaster Tools** isn't verified with the connected Google account.
To stop, choose **Disconnect**; SendLit deletes the stored access and data.
You can also remove SendLit's access in your Google account's security
settings.

On a self-hosted SendLit, an administrator first creates a Google OAuth client
and sets `GOOGLE_POSTMASTER_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_POSTMASTER_CLIENT_SECRET`;
see `.env.example`.

## Connect Microsoft SNDS

1. Sign in to [SNDS](https://substrate.office.com/ip-domain-management-snds/SNDS)
   and request access to your sending IPs (**Request Access**). Microsoft
   emails the address responsible for each IP to confirm.
2. Open **Automated Data Access**, enable it and copy the data link.
3. Paste the link under **Outlook · Microsoft SNDS** on the dashboard and
   choose **Connect**. SendLit downloads the data once to check the link.

SNDS links expire after 30 days. When yours does, the card says so; paste the
new link from the same page.

## API and MCP

`GET /reputation` returns the same data and alerts, and `POST /reputation/sync`
refreshes it. Connecting Google returns a sign-in URL from
`POST /reputation/google/connect` that a person opens in a browser. SNDS is
connected with `PUT /reputation/snds`. See the [API reference](/api-reference)
and the `*_reputation` MCP tools.
