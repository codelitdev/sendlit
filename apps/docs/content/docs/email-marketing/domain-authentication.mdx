title: Domain authentication
description: Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC so your email reaches the inbox.
---

Mailbox providers check three DNS records to decide whether mail really comes
from the domain in its From address. Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require all
three from anyone sending more than a few thousand emails a day, and mail
without them is likely to be rejected or land in spam.

| Record | What it proves |
| --- | --- |
| **SPF** | The server sending the mail is allowed to send for the domain. |
| **DKIM** | The message was signed by the domain and not changed on the way. |
| **DMARC** | What receivers should do with mail that fails SPF and DKIM, and where to send reports. |

You add these records at your DNS provider (Cloudflare, Route 53, your
registrar, ...). Your email provider generates the DKIM keys; SendLit tells
you which records are missing and checks them for you.

## Check a domain

In **Settings → Email service providers**, press the shield button on an ESP.
SendLit looks up the DNS of its From address's domain and shows:

- whether SPF, DKIM and DMARC are **Set up**, **Need attention** or are
  **Missing**, and why;
- the DNS records to add, with copy buttons and whether each one was found.
  Values only your provider knows, such as DKIM keys, are marked "From your
  provider", with a link to its console.

Press **Check again** after you change DNS; changes can take a few hours to
show up. If your provider signs with its own DKIM selector (the part before
`._domainkey`), enter it to check that key. SendLit otherwise tries your
provider's selectors and common ones.

The same check is available as `GET /settings/esps/:espId/domain-authentication`
in the [API](/api-reference) and as the `check_esp_domain_authentication` MCP
tool.

## What each provider needs

| Provider | SPF | DKIM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon SES | Custom MAIL FROM domain (SendLit suggests `mail.<domain>`): `MX 10 feedback-smtp.<region>.amazonses.com` and `TXT v=spf1 include:amazonses.com ~all` | Three Easy DKIM `CNAME` records from the SES console |
| Resend | `send.<domain>`: the same `MX` and `TXT` records as SES | `resend._domainkey` TXT record |
| Postmark | `CNAME pm-bounces.<domain> → pm.mtasv.net` | `<date>pm._domainkey` TXT record |
| SendGrid | Handled by its `em<id>` CNAME | `s1._domainkey` and `s2._domainkey` CNAMEs |
| Mailgun | `include:mailgun.org` in your domain's SPF record | TXT record at the selector Mailgun shows |
| Other SMTP | Your mail server's SPF record | Your mail server's DKIM selector |

For SES, SendLit reads the region from the SMTP host
(`email-smtp.<region>.amazonaws.com`). SES names its DKIM records with random
tokens, so SendLit can't find them on its own: paste one token (or a whole
`<token>._domainkey.<domain>` name) from the SES console's DKIM section into
**Easy DKIM token** and press **Check again**.

A domain may have only **one** SPF record. If you already have one (for
example for Google Workspace), add the provider's `include:` to it instead of
creating a second; SendLit suggests the merged record.

## Start DMARC with monitoring

If you have no DMARC record, start with:

```
_dmarc.example.com  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com"
```

`p=none` changes nothing about delivery; receivers just send reports to the
`rua` address. Once the reports show that only your own services send as your
domain, move to `p=quarantine` and later `p=reject`. A DMARC record on your
main domain also covers subdomains such as `news.example.com`.
